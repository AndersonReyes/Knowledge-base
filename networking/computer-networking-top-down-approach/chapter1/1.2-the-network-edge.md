# 1.2 The Network Edge

## End Systems (Hosts)

- Sit at the **edge** of the Internet
- Run application programs (browsers, servers, email clients)
- Divided into **clients** (desktops, laptops, phones) and **servers** (data centers)
- Google (as of 2020): 19 data centers on 4 continents, millions of servers

## 1.2.1 Access Networks

**Access network** = physical network connecting an end system to the **first/edge router** on the path to any other end system.

### Home Access

#### DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)
- Uses existing **telephone line** to telco's central office (CO)
- Customer's DSL modem ↔ DSLAM (DSL Access Multiplexer) at CO
- Single telephone line carries 3 simultaneous channels via FDM:
  - `0–4 kHz` — ordinary phone call
  - `4–50 kHz` — upstream data (medium speed)
  - `50 kHz–1 MHz` — downstream data (high speed)
- **Asymmetric** (downstream > upstream):
  - Downstream: 24 Mbps / 52 Mbps (standard), up to 1 Gbps (newest ITU 2014 standard)
  - Upstream: 3.5 Mbps / 16 Mbps
- Max distance: ~5–10 miles from CO
- Hundreds/thousands of households connect to single DSLAM

#### Cable Internet (HFC — Hybrid Fiber-Coaxial)
- Uses cable TV company's existing infrastructure
- Fiber from **cable head end** → neighborhood junctions; then **coaxial cable** to homes
- Each junction typically serves 500–5,000 homes
- Requires **cable modem** (external device, connects via Ethernet)
- **CMTS** (Cable Modem Termination System) at head end — analogous to DSLAM
- **Asymmetric** (downstream > upstream):
  - DOCSIS 2.0: downstream 40 Mbps, upstream 30 Mbps
  - DOCSIS 3.0: downstream 1.2 Gbps, upstream 100 Mbps
- **Shared broadcast medium** — all packets from head end go to every home downstream; upstream is shared too → requires distributed multiple-access protocol to avoid collisions

#### FTTH (Fiber to the Home)
- Optical fiber path directly from CO to home
- Potential speeds: gigabits per second range
- Two optical distribution architectures:
  - **AON (Active Optical Network)** — essentially switched Ethernet
  - **PON (Passive Optical Network)** — used in Verizon FiOS:
    - Each home has ONT (Optical Network Terminator)
    - Dedicated fiber from ONT → neighborhood splitter
    - Splitter combines <100 homes onto single shared fiber → OLT (Optical Line Terminator) at CO
    - OLT converts optical↔electrical signals, connects to Internet via telco router
    - Packets from OLT replicated at splitter (like cable head end)

#### 5G Fixed Wireless
- No cabling from CO to home
- Uses beam-forming technology from provider's base station to modem in home
- WiFi router connected to modem (similar to DSL/cable setup)

### Enterprise / Home LAN

#### Ethernet
- Most prevalent access technology in corporate/university/home networks
- Twisted-pair copper wire → Ethernet switch
- User speeds: 100 Mbps to tens of Gbps to switch
- Server speeds: 1 Gbps to 10 Gbps

#### WiFi (IEEE 802.11)
- Wireless LAN: packets to/from **access point** connected to wired Ethernet
- User must be within **tens of meters** of access point
- Shared transmission rate: >100 Mbps (modern 802.11)

### Wide-Area Wireless (Cellular)

- Same infrastructure as cellular telephony
- User within **tens of kilometers** of base station (vs. tens of meters for WiFi)
- **4G:** real-world download speeds up to ~60 Mbps
- **5G:** next-gen, higher speeds, being deployed

## 1.2.2 Physical Media

### Two Categories

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Guided media** | Waves guided along solid medium | Fiber-optic, twisted-pair copper, coaxial cable |
| **Unguided media** | Waves propagate freely | Wireless LAN, satellite |

> **Note:** Labor cost of installation >> cost of material, so builders often install multiple media types preemptively.

### Twisted-Pair Copper Wire
- Cheapest, most widely used guided medium
- >99% of wired telephone connections to local switch use it
- Two insulated ~1mm copper wires in regular spiral — reduces electrical interference
- **UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)** — common in LANs
- Speeds: 10 Mbps – 10 Gbps (Cat 6a: 10 Gbps up to 100m)
- DSL uses twisted pair for residential access

### Coaxial Cable
- Two concentric copper conductors with insulation/shielding
- High data transmission rates
- Common in cable TV/Internet systems
- Used as **guided shared medium** — multiple end systems can connect directly

### Fiber Optics
- Conducts pulses of light; each pulse = 1 bit
- Supports tens–hundreds of Gbps
- **Immune to electromagnetic interference**
- Very low signal attenuation up to 100 km
- Hard to tap — preferred for long-haul and undersea links
- **OC-n standard speeds:** OC-n = n × 51.8 Mbps (OC-1 through OC-768)
- High cost of optical devices limits short-haul/residential deployment

### Terrestrial Radio

| Range | Distance | Examples |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Short-distance | 1–2 meters | Wireless headsets, keyboards, medical devices |
| Local area | 10–hundreds of meters | WiFi (802.11) |
| Wide area | tens of km | Cellular (4G/5G) |

- No physical wire; penetrates walls; supports mobile users
- Subject to: path loss, shadow fading, multipath fading, interference

### Satellite Radio
- Links two or more Earth-based ground stations
- Receives on one frequency, regenerates, retransmits on another (via repeater)

| Type | Altitude | RTT delay | Notes |
|------|----------|-----------|-------|
| **Geostationary (GEO)** | 36,000 km | ~280 ms one-way | Stationary above same Earth spot |
| **LEO** | Much lower | Less delay | Rotate around Earth; need many for coverage |

- GEO speeds: hundreds of Mbps; used where DSL/cable unavailable
